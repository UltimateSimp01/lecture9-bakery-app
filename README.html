Bakery Order Desk user guide

The baker’s day starts on Monday, November 23, 2026, with 13 orders already booked. Use seven days of Instagram messages to decide which orders to take, track pickup revenue, and allocate oven time. Closing the browser preserves your progress.

Run a business day

  1. Extract the entire bakery folder to a writable location. On Windows, double-click Open Bakery Workbench.vbs to open that folder’s saved business day in your browser.
  2. Check the pickup dates under Oven capacity. If a helper’s four hours should count when new requests are checked, select Book helper before fetching the messages. The reminder below the day’s action buttons includes a Review oven capacity link.
  3. Select Get today’s messages. The desk processes changes and cancellations and shows why any request was rejected. For a pending new order, compare the hours it needs with the time left on the pickup day. Select Accept or Reject, review the details, then confirm or choose Go back. The outcome appears on that request.
  4. Select Accept remaining & close day when ready. After you confirm, the desk checks each remaining request in arrival order, records that day’s pickups at 5 p.m., and advances one day. The closing message counts all approved and declined requests received that day and reports the number of pickups separately.
  5. Sunday has the final messages. Some customers collect on Monday, November 30, or Tuesday, December 1. Open Weekly totals to review either week as the simulation progresses. You can continue closing pickup days through December 1.

Order and accounting rules

RuleHow the desk applies it
ProductsEach order contains one product. One dozen cupcakes costs $40 and needs 30 minutes. One cake costs $120 and needs two hours.
Two days’ noticeCount calendar days from the message or helper booking to pickup. A Monday request for Wednesday meets the rule. It applies to orders, changes, cancellations, and helper bookings.
CapacityAll baking happens on the pickup day. The oven has eight hours a day. One helper costs $100 for that day and raises capacity to 12 hours. A pending request reserves no hours. The desk rechecks available time when a request is accepted and continues after any order that will not fit.
Changes and cancellationsMatch by customer and pickup date. A change counts the difference in oven time. A cancellation releases the original hours. An unsuccessful request leaves the original order intact.
MoneyOrders earn revenue when picked up. Confirmed orders awaiting pickup have a separate balance. A helper booking records the cost once. Weekly totals group earned revenue by pickup date and committed helper costs by the helper’s work date. Cancelled and rejected orders earn no revenue.

Design and data

app.py serves the page and runs the business rules locally with Python’s standard library. It reads existing_orders.csv and the seven daily Instagram HTML files. SQLite keeps orders, requests, and progress in bakery.db. The app works offline.

Actions and the records they change
ActionWhat changes
Fetch messagesSave each request and its decision. Apply eligible changes or cancellations to the matching order. Mark the day as fetched only after the whole inbox is read.
Accept or rejectSave the decision. Accepting creates a booked order after a fresh capacity check. Rejecting leaves oven time available.
Book helperAfter checking notice, record one booking against the work date. Its four hours and $100 cost become visible together.
Close dayCheck remaining requests in sequence, mark due orders as picked up, and move the business-day record forward or finish the simulation.

How the desk saves an action

Fetch, order decisions, helper bookings, and day closure each use a database transaction. A successful fetch imports a day once. The desk compares the source file’s stated message count with the complete messages it read. An incomplete import leaves the inbox, orders, and version unchanged. Restore the complete source file and fetch again.

Each button submits the version the baker saw. The server checks it before saving, so two tabs cannot process the same order or close two days in a race. Restarting the app restores the last saved state.

Follow-up review · two rounds

In round one, the three reviewers compared the running desk with the lecture. They reproduced a day-close message that omitted earlier decisions and a truncated inbox that could appear fully loaded. They also found that the cumulative revenue total did not identify an earlier week after the simulation had moved on.

In round two, they cross-checked the day summary against all messages received, weekly revenue against pickup dates, and file completeness against the count in each source. The design now reports full-day decisions, keeps weekly figures available, and allows a failed import to be retried. A reminder beside the fetch controls explains when capacity is checked, individual acceptance has a confirmation step, and feedback appears beside the affected request or helper booking.

The usability review used simulated baker tasks on copies of the course database. Live Instagram intake, actual pickup confirmations, and observation of working bakers would provide separate evidence for a real bakery.

Without a helper, the desk approved 13 new orders, one change, and two cancellations. Of the nine requests it declined, five gave too little notice and four exceeded oven capacity. Twenty orders had been picked up by Sunday evening. Four orders worth $640 were still due.

In the third scenario, manually rejecting Jasmine and accepting Patel reduced the week’s pickup revenue to $2,880. In the fourth, booking Saturday’s helper on Thursday met the two-day rule and produced $3,240 in pickup revenue. Earlier rejections kept their original decisions.

Three checks a reviewer can reproduce

  • On the original Monday, Friday has three oven hours left. At the end of the no-helper replay, Friday has used all eight.
  • On Monday, reject Jasmine and accept Patel. Okafor is rejected automatically. Close the day. The summary should show one approved request, two declined requests, and two pickups from orders booked before Monday.
  • Without a helper, close November 30 and December 1. All 24 active orders have now been picked up. The first week retains $3,000 in revenue. The second week shows $640 recorded through December 1. Cumulative earned revenue is $3,640, with $0 awaiting pickup.

The follow-up tests passed on separate database copies. A Monday file cut off during its third message saved zero messages. Restoring that file and trying again loaded all three exactly once. Separate bookings for helpers on Saturday and the following Monday placed one $100 cost in each work week.

In a phone-sized browser, cancelling an acceptance kept the request pending, confirming a decision kept the original message open, and booking a helper showed feedback at the selected date. The regression checks also covered duplicate clicks, stale submissions, restart recovery, and the month boundary.
